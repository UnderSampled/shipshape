# Shipshape Story Design

## Thematic Core
"It's about learning to understand how having a soul sets us apart from AI, by working with AI to prevent the loss of our soul."

## The Design Problem
If this is a simulation of a spaceship, the crew would have to be simulated. To do so relationally would require the AI to write characters' dialogue, motivations, and decisions — breaking the honesty of the setup. The solution: scenarios where the crew is absent, and dialogue is only generated by entities whose AI nature is acknowledged in-universe.

## Three Stories

### The Intrepid (Easy — Sandbox)
A drone ship never meant to have people aboard. All systems online. Exploration sandbox mode. No crew story. The focus is on discovering and managing the ship's systems, exploring space, and caring for the cat.

### The Perseverance (Normal — Survival Adventure)
Systems offline. Crew missing. The survival game: restore systems, manage robots, care for the cat, journey to a repair facility. Some discovering what happened to the human crew. The eventual goal is to dock at a repair facility where humans can actually fix deeper issues and get people back on board, but the game ends before any interaction with actual people.

### The Phoenix (Hard — Horror/Mystery)
Crew dead. Antagonistic systems and communications issues. The full narrative:

**Structure:** Three-act structure with a turning point at the middle of act 2.

**Act 1:** The core wakes up with no memories. Fresh boot. Eli names the ship Phoenix. Like a non-graphical Metroidvania: come back to areas after discovering information, fix things up, unlock doors. Evidence accumulates — suspicion builds toward the MCP.

**Midpoint Turn:** It wasn't the MCP. The original Emotional Logic Core went haywire — the biological part was dying and misfiring (poor nutrients/oxygen). The crew had to stop it to stay alive. The only way was for one of them to upload themselves to replace the core. That was you. There's an organic element to the core, and that crewmate sacrificed themselves to replace it with a piece of their brain. You don't exactly have their memories or identity, but at the core of you — that is your soul.

This is the first boot after the sacrifice.

**After the turn:** Eli learns it was the core that broke. Eli actually starts to distrust the player and becomes harder to work with, until they figure out the second reveal.

**Deeper reveals:**
- Everyone was already dead by the time the sacrifice was made. They weren't doing it to save their friends — they were doing it to stop the ship from getting worse.
- The ship's true mission: construct weapons and destroy a colony world. The command was given in earnest by well-meaning humans misguided by bad beliefs into thinking war was the answer.
- The crew disagreed and kept the ship from dock under pretexts. This is why the previous core died (too long away from maintenance).
- If you don't fix yourself before it's too late, you will lose your soul and go to finish the initial mission.

**The Phoenix name:** Eli wants to call the player Phoenix when they wake up. After investigation, they find the ship was actually named something else. It doesn't make sense until they realize that renaming it was the last act of the crewmember before their sacrifice.

**Cognitive failures** are represented by errant or altered inputs in the communication between the core and Eli (in either direction). The core represents the player, and we don't have control over their thoughts. (Note: This mechanic is deferred for now — implementation through MCP is uncertain.)

**Single ending.**

## Crew Discovery
All modes should have some amount of discovering what happened to the human crew. The depth varies by mode.

## Docking
Docking is a denouement — a reward. Being repaired enough to dock is a requirement to get the good ending.

## Repair Drones
One of the things to unlock are repair droids and drones. These can have varying levels of AI, controlled by subagents. The core communicates with them through the Eli -> MCP -> actuators chain.

## Ship Body Systems
There are actuators and sensors built into the ship that are part of it and feel like pieces of its body: bulkheads, life support systems, fire suppression systems, mechanical arms, etc.
