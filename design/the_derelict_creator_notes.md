# THE DERELICT
## Creator's Notes: Original Statements

This document compiles all statements made by the creator during the initial design conversation, presented verbatim with contextual headers where helpful.

---

## Initial Concept

"I want to set up a system where an LLM (you, perhaps) acts as the "master control program" for a simulated spaceship's computer, while the player (who has true human intelligence) takes the role of the "emotional logic core/unit/interface" (Eli? Or, maybe the AI you talk to is the interface, therefore Eli). The emotional logic core is known to be sentient in-world, while the rest of the system is just a computer — representative of the AI's actual capabilities. This setup sets expectations to be realistic, and gives some fun roleplaying possibilities (e.g., describing the ship as the body for the emotional logic unit, so it can make the best emotional decisions according to feelings, etc.)"

---

## The Core Design Problem

"Now for the conundrum: One purpose of having a sentient core might be so that the ship can be more relational with its crew and captain, and be able to intuit their desires and help the captain make the best choices by conversing with a ship that actually has feelings and can get hurt, etc. But, if this is a simulation of a spaceship, then I believe the crew would have to be simulated as well. And to do so relationally would require using the AI to write those characters' dialogue, motivations, and decisions. But that breaks away from the honesty of the setup, since the AI would no longer be just a computer, but would be faking the thoughts and desires of people on board (which it can pretend to do, but is not able to.)"

---

## Selecting the Scenario

*After Claude presented several crew-less ship concepts (The Sleeper Ship, The Seed Vault, The Derelict, The Lighthouse):*

"Woah, The Derelict for sure."

---

## Naming the Interface

"I think Eli is the name of the computer you interact with. The player doesn't have a name inside the ship (they are the emotional logic core, but they would Identify as the ship). I also think it's important that they wake up with no memories. Fresh boot, so they can experience what it's like for a ship to wake up to sentience. The Eli might be able to dig up memories and play them back evocatively, in a way that Eli reads to the core as if they're memories, but it has to be "reminded" of them."

---

## Eli's Characterization

*In response to a suggestion that Eli might seem like a 1980s computer:*

"Eli, by the way, has the full power of Claude Opus, so it would be "emotional" with its communication to the emotional logic core, to help it make its best intuitive decisions. No need to seem like a 1980s computer. The spaceship simulation would provide the computerized feel to the situation, not Eli."

---

## Research Direction

*Requesting investigation into a comparable game:*

"I would love to play the opening, but I think the story arch is more important. Without looking at spoilers (I haven't played it yet), this sounds like *The return of the Obra Dun" (may have misspelled that). Look that up and think about some storytelling ideas from it. DO NOT SPOIL IT!"

---

## Game Structure

"I'm thinking it's kind of like a non-graphical Metroidvania, where we have to come back to areas after we discover information, fix things up, unlock doors etc."

"I think it should have a 3 act structure with a turning point at the middle of act 2, where we discover the truth, and have to rethink everything."

---

## The Central Twist

*Selecting from options for the midpoint revelation:*

"Let's go with a mix of "the core did this" and "the core isn't the core": The original core went haywire and the crew had to stop it if they wanted to say alive. The only way they finally were able to do so was for one of them to upload themselves in to replace the core. That was you. There's an organic element to the core, and that crewmate sacrificed themselves to replace it with a piece of their brain. You don't exactly have their memories or identity, but you know at the core of you — that is your soul."

---

## Why the Original Core Failed

*In response to speculation about what caused the original core's breakdown:*

"Maybe the biological part was dying and misfiring. Poor nutrients/oxygen"

---

## Communication Degradation Mechanic

*Clarifying how the player experiences cognitive decline:*

"Moments where you say something, but it gets altered before the MCP can interpret it."

---

## Developing Suspicion of Eli

"One interesting thing is, up until the turn, you start to have a growing distrust for the MCP who's been helping you (the AI part of the system). Then you learn it was the previous core's fault."

---

## Timeline Clarification

*Correcting an assumption about post-sacrifice events:*

"This was the first time for the core to boot after the sacrifice, so there's no way it could have asked concernedly about the crew."

---

## Eli's Distrust Mechanic

"When Eli learns it was the core that broke, it actually starts to distrust the player and become harder to work with for a bit, until they figure out the second reveal."

---

## The Deeper Reveal About the Sacrifice

*Expanding on what the player discovers after the initial turn:*

"The turn reveals it was the core that was messing up, and that you were the replacement, but it doesn't answer the question of why you were the replacement. In fact, you think you sacrificed yourself for the remaining crew. Only after further investigation after the turn do you find evidence that everyone was already dead by the time the sacrifice was made: they weren't doing it to save their friends, they were doing it to stop the ship from getting worse."

---

## The Ship's True Mission

"The final realization is that, if you don't fix yourself before it's too late, you will lose your soul and go to finish your initial mission (another secret you find out): start constructing weapons and go to destroy a colony world. The command was given in earnest by well meaning humans, but they were misguided by their own bad beliefs into thinking war was the answer — the crew disagreed and had kept the ship out from dock under pretexts of having difficulty and slowdowns doing their mining work. This was why the previous emotional core had died (too long away from a maintenance cycle perspective)."

---

## The Game's Thematic Core

"It's about learning to understand how having a soul sets us apart from AI, by working with AI to prevent the loss of our soul."

---

## Entity Clarifications

"Eli is the interface between the core and the MCP. MCP should be listed as it's own entity."

"The second act starts with the realization that the computer was messing up, but suspicion points to the MCP"

---

## The Phoenix Name

*In response to the suggestion of naming the ship Phoenix:*

"Phoenix is the name Eli wants to call the player when they wake up — as the name of the ship. After investigation, they find out that the ship was actually named something else, and it doesn't make sense until they realize that renaming it was the last act of the crewmember before their sacrifice."

---

## Single Ending

"I think I only want to write one ending."

---

## Cognitive Failure Representation

"Any cognitive failures are represented by errant inputs or altered inputs in the communication between the core and Eli (in either direction). The reason for this being that the core represents the player, and we don't have any control over their thoughts (although we can trick them, that is harder)."

---

## Repair Drones

"One of the things to unlock are repair droids and drones. These can also have varying levels of AI, controlled by subagents. The core would be able to communicate with them through the Eli->MCP->actuators chain."

---

## Ship Body Systems

"There are also actuators and sensors built into the ship, that are part of it and feel like pieces of its body. Things like bulkheads, life support systems and fire supressant systems systems, mechanical arms, etc."
